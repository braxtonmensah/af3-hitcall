**Subject:** Free screening enquiry — interface-directed compounds from a structure-based screen

---

Dear CO-ADD team,

I would like to ask whether a small set of compounds would be suitable for your free primary screening.

I have identified a candidate antibacterial target in *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*: an essential 2:2
heterotetramer of RNase J (MPN280) and MPN621, a catalytically dead paralog that appears to act as a
structural subunit. Both genes are essential by transposon mutagenesis. I mapped the protein-protein
interface from structural models and am selecting approved compounds predicted to bind it by
structure-based virtual screening.

I understand your primary panel covers the ESKAPE pathogens and two fungi rather than Mycoplasma. My
question is whether you would still accept ~10-20 approved compounds selected this way, on the basis
that they were not designed as antibiotics and would not otherwise be screened for antimicrobial
activity. I can supply 1 mg of each at the purity you specify.

I am an undergraduate researcher working independently, and I am happy to share the structural models
and the pre-registered analysis behind the selection.

Thank you for considering this.

Braxton Mensah
bsmensah@iu.edu

## Notes

- CO-ADD's stated remit is compounds "not designed as antibiotics that would not otherwise be screened",
  which approved drugs repurposed against a novel interface fit well.
- They make no IP claims and provide a confidentiality period before results go into their open
  database, so you can publish or patent a hit first.
- 1 mg per compound is a small, cheap quantity: most suppliers' smallest vial exceeds it.
