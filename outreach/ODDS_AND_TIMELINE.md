# Timeline and odds of clearing $50k (2026-09-25)

My honest estimates. The probabilities are judgement calls, not measurements, and I have tried to be
neither promotional nor falsely modest. Where I am guessing, it says so.

## Timeline

| When | What happens | Money in hand |
|---|---|---|
| **Oct 2026** | Preprint + repo public. Emergent Ventures submitted. SAM.gov + LLC + EIN started. | $0 |
| **Nov 2026** | eRA Commons, SBC ID, Grants.gov done. Boltz-2 clean-room run (~$30). SBIR drafting. | $0 |
| **Dec 2026 - Jan 2027** | Possible Emergent Ventures decision (rolling, often weeks). | **$0-50k** |
| **Jan 5 2027** | **SBIR Phase I submitted.** | |
| Feb - Jun 2027 | Peer review, then council. Astera / Speculative Technologies applications. | |
| **Jul - Sep 2027** | SBIR decision. Award start typically ~9 months after submission. | **$0 or ~$323k** |
| Apr 2027 | CARB-X EOI window, if wet-lab confirmation has landed. | |

So: small money possible within **3 months**, the large money lands around **12 months** out. If the
January deadline slips (most often because SAM.gov was started late), everything shifts to the April 5
deadline and award moves to early 2028.

## Odds of clearing $50k

| Route | Amount if hit | P(hit) | Basis for the number |
|---|---|---|---|
| NIH SBIR Phase I | ~$323k | **10-12%** | NIH Phase I success is roughly 15-20% overall; I discount it for a first-time applicant, no PhD, no institutional track record, no wet lab. |
| Astera / Speculative Technologies / similar | $50k+ | ~5% | Small, idiosyncratic programmes; they do fund independent researchers, but rarely undergraduates. |
| IndieBio | $250-525k | ~2% | ~60 funded from thousands of applications, and normally a team. |
| Emergent Ventures | usually $5-25k | ~20-25% to get *something* | Good fit for the profile. Clearing $50k from EV alone is unlikely, ~3%. |
| Consulting / services | $50k needs 300-500 billable hours | ~5% | Not realistic alongside full-time school. |

**P(at least one outcome above $50k within 18 months) ≈ 20%.**
**P(at least some money, $5-25k) ≈ 50-60%**, driven almost entirely by Emergent Ventures.
**Expected value ≈ $50-60k**, but that average is misleading: the distribution is one big unlikely hit
plus a likely small grant. The median outcome is roughly $10-15k.

## What actually moves the odds

1. **Wet-lab confirmation of the 2:2 stoichiometry.** Mass photometry on a purified sample is cheap
   fee-for-service work. It converts "predicted and consistent with published data" into "measured",
   and it is the difference between a plausible SBIR and a strong one. Biggest single lever.
2. **A co-author with a track record.** Adding an established researcher to the SBIR roughly doubles
   credibility with reviewers. Costs you sole ownership, so it is a real trade.
3. **Submitting on time.** Start SAM.gov this week or the January deadline is gone.
4. **The preprint being public.** Every funder on the list checks whether the work is real.

## What does not move the odds

- Patents. Nothing here is patentable, so filing anything is wasted money.
- More computation on the same complex. Five independent lines already agree; a sixth adds little.
- Talking to pharma about licensing. There is no licensable asset without IP.

## The honest summary

You have a genuinely good scientific result and a real, legal path to six-figure funding. But the
probability of clearing $50k in the next 18 months is about **one in five**, most of the value sits in a
single competitive grant decided next summer, and the most likely single outcome is a small grant of
$10-15k. If you need money sooner or more reliably than that, it has to come from somewhere other than
this project.
